Grouper design system: design board

Navy, not grey. Depth by tone, then hairline. Glass on chrome only. Teal is the one action colour, with dark ink on the fill; purple is the second brand accent. Colour means identity or state, never decoration.

Status: proposal, 27 November 2026. No code was changed to write this. It develops the direction in docs/gui-concept-plan.md (26 November) and does not replace it: where a value already exists in crates/hub/web/app.css it is kept unless a table below says otherwise and gives the reason. The safety, accessibility and routing contracts in docs/gui-spec.md and the data rules in docs/usage-tab-spec.md still stand.

Principles and anti-patterns

Principles

  1. Colour means identity or state, never decoration. A hue on screen answers "which model", "whose bill" or "what state". If it answers none, it is navy.
  2. One dominant element per screen. The composer in a chat, the total and donut on Usage, the dock during an approval. Everything else steps back a tier.
  3. One filled teal action per region. Teal is where the next tap goes.
  4. Glass is chrome only. If text has to be read carefully or a decision is made on it, the surface is opaque.
  5. The name is the fact, the colour is the cue. Every model, key and state is named in text.
  6. Depth by tone and hairline. Shadows mean "this floats and will go away".
  7. Mono means exact. If the owner might copy it or compare it digit by digit, it is mono or tabular. If it is prose, it is not.
  8. Quiet until it matters. Events are lines, not cards; approvals are the one thing that takes the screen.
  9. Same product on every device. Phone removes columns, not components.

Anti-patterns

  • Purple-gradient everything. The brand gradient is for the mark and one line of brand text. No gradient buttons, cards, borders, charts or backgrounds behind body text.
  • A blue wash: a dark screenshot with only navy and teal fails the squint test (V2). So does a rainbow: more than one hue that means nothing.
  • Family or key colour on a control. Buttons are teal, outline or danger, never orange because the thread is Llama.
  • Warn or danger used as an identity colour, or amber and rose identity shown without their mark.
  • Glass cards, glass tables, blur behind the approval reason or tool input.
  • White text on the teal fill (2.2:1).
  • A second meaning for the focus blue. An outline in blue is always focus.
  • New font sizes, weights, radii or durations outside the tables in section 2.
  • Capsule status pills, shadows under dark cards, illustration in working screens, dead buttons.
  • Uppercase mono labels on sentences, or mono for prose. It is a label style, not a theme.

7. Adoption plan

Aliases first, values second, components last. Steps 1–2 are safe to merge at any time; steps 3, 5 and 6 are the ones the owner will notice; step 9 can wait or be dropped item by item.

8. Decisions for the owner

  1. A voice typeface? Recommend: no. Use one line in the brand gradient as Grouper's voice (sign-in, new chat, first-run empty states). A serif or display face would need a bundled font to look the same on Windows, iPhone and Mac, and would compete with the mark.
  2. Mono face: system or bundled? Recommend: system (SF Mono on Apple, Cascadia on Windows). It costs nothing and matches each platform. Bundling one face (about 100 KB, open licence) would make IDs and the status line look identical everywhere; worth it only if the difference bothers you once you see both.
  3. Light theme: full parity or dark-first? Recommend: token parity, dark-first design. Every token and component ships in both themes and must pass the same contrast checks, but new components are designed and screenshotted in dark, and brand imagery stays dark.
  4. The desktop status line. It is a new permanent strip on desktop. Recommend: build it, with a switch in Settings, because subscription headroom is what you check most and it saves a trip to Usage. The alternative is to keep the window as it is and rely on the Usage page and widgets.
  5. Teal and purple each do two jobs. Teal is the action colour and the Gemini family; purple is the brand highlight and the Qwen family. Recommend: keep both, with the rule that brand teal and purple appear only on controls, counts and progress, and family colour only on marks, dots and running pills, always beside the model's name. The alternative is to move those two families to other hues, which breaks the concept art's own thread colours.
  6. Seven proposed solid colours. Teal, purple and orange solids come from your art; the other seven (pink, blue, green, amber, rose, indigo, cyan) are mine. Recommend: accept them for step 3 and adjust by eye on a real donut with all ten keys; they are one line each to change.
